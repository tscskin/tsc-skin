## 1. Purpose

TSC Skin provides paediatric dermatology, including diagnosis, laser treatment and minor
skin surgery for patients under 18. This policy sets out who may consent to that care, who
must be present, and how enquiries concerning young patients are handled.

It reflects GMC guidance *0–18 years: guidance for all doctors* and the principle that
young people should be involved in decisions about their own care to the greatest extent
they are able.

## 2. Key principles

1. The welfare of the child is the primary consideration in every decision.
2. Young people are presumed to want to be involved in decisions about their own care, and
   should be given information in a way they can understand.
3. Competence is assessed for the specific decision in question, not in general. A young
   person may be competent to consent to a straightforward treatment but not to a complex
   or irreversible one.
4. Where a competent young person and a parent disagree, senior advice and, where
   necessary, legal advice should be sought before proceeding.

## 3. Who may give consent

**Patients aged 16 and 17** are presumed in law to be capable of consenting to their own
treatment. Consent from a person with parental responsibility is not required, although
young people should be encouraged to involve their family. If a 16 or 17 year old lacks
capacity for the decision, a person with parental responsibility may consent on their
behalf.

**Patients under 16** may consent for themselves if the treating clinician assesses them as
having sufficient understanding and maturity to understand what is proposed — Gillick
competence. This assessment is made for each decision and recorded in the notes. Where the
young person is not competent for that decision, consent must be obtained from a person
with parental responsibility.

**A person with parental responsibility** means: the mother; the father if married to the
mother at the time of birth or named on the birth certificate (for births registered in
England and Wales from 1 December 2003); a legal guardian; a person with a child
arrangements or special guardianship order; or a local authority with a care order.

## 4. Attendance at appointments

- Patients **under 16** must be accompanied by a person with parental responsibility, or by
  an adult who has that person's documented authority to attend.
- Patients aged **16 and 17** may attend alone. They are encouraged to bring a parent,
  guardian or other supportive adult, particularly for procedures.
- For any **procedure under local anaesthetic, laser treatment or minor surgery** on a
  patient under 18, an accompanying adult must be present at the clinic and available
  afterwards. Where a host clinic sets a stricter requirement, that requirement applies.
- A chaperone is offered for every intimate examination and for examination of a young
  patient, and the offer and outcome are recorded.

## 5. Website enquiries about young patients

Where an age under 18 is entered on the website enquiry form, the enquirer is asked whether
they are the patient or are enquiring on the patient's behalf, and if the latter, their name
and relationship to the patient.

- Where an adult enquires on behalf of a child, we correspond with that adult and confirm
  parental responsibility before the first appointment.
- Where a young person aged **13 to 15** enquires about themselves, we respond briefly and
  explain that a parent or guardian must be involved before an appointment can be confirmed.
  We do not discuss clinical detail with them until that adult is engaged.
- Where an enquiry appears to come from a patient **under 13**, we do not correspond with the
  child. We reply asking that a parent or guardian contacts us directly, and take no further
  action until they do.
- Where a young person aged 16 or 17 enquires about themselves, we correspond with them
  directly, and encourage them to involve a parent or other supportive adult.
- We do not send marketing communications to anyone identified as under 18.

## 6. Recording consent

For every patient under 18 the record should show:

- who gave consent, and on what basis (self, Gillick-competent, or parental responsibility)
- the relationship of any adult consenting, and how parental responsibility was established
- what was explained, including benefits, risks, alternatives and expected outcome
- for a competent young person, that their own views were sought and recorded
- who was present at the appointment, including any chaperone

Written consent is obtained for all procedures, and for laser treatment. The specific consent
requirements for an individual patient's treatment plan are determined at the initial
consultation and recorded in the notes.

## 7. Refusal and disagreement

A refusal of treatment by a competent young person under 18 may in some circumstances be
overridden by a person with parental responsibility or by a court, but this is a significant
step. Where a competent young person refuses treatment that is considered to be in their
interests, treatment should not proceed without senior and, where appropriate, legal advice.

## 8. Confidentiality

Young people are owed the same duty of confidentiality as adults. Information should not be
disclosed to a parent without the young person's agreement where they are competent, unless
disclosure is necessary to protect them or another person from serious harm.

## 9. Safeguarding

Any concern that a child may be at risk of harm is acted on immediately, in line with the
safeguarding policy of the clinic where the patient is seen, and escalated to the relevant
local safeguarding team.
